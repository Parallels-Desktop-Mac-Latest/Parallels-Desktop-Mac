## 1. Say what the machine is for, out loud

Not which operating system — what the work is. Office documents and a line-of-business application is one
answer; a games library is another; creative software with enormous assets is a third; compilers and test
environments a fourth. Those four are exactly the tuning profiles offered later, so naming the job now means
step four takes one click instead of an evening of trial and error.

## 2. Choose the source: existing partition or clean build

If Boot Camp is already on the disk, that is your starting point. Converting it is the intended route and
keeps everything that was installed inside it, so the licences you activated years ago stay activated. If
there is no partition, you are building from scratch — slower, but a chance to leave out the accumulated
clutter of a system that has been running since 2019.

## 3. Decide which system is going in

Windows is the common answer, in the 10, 8.1 or 7 flavour depending on what your holdout application demands.
Linux is the answer for developers who want an isolated box. macOS as a guest is the answer for testing or
for keeping an old environment usable. Chrome is the answer when the point is a minimal, disposable place to
open something you do not trust. One machine can hold several of these; they simply do not have to.

## 4. Apply the profile instead of guessing at numbers

With the workload named, pick productivity, games, design or development and the guest's settings and
performance are adjusted for that shape of work in a single action. Resist the urge to override it
immediately. Manual tuning is worth doing when you have a measured complaint, not on day one when the only
evidence available is a feeling.

## 5. Choose how visible the other system stays

Two arrangements, and neither is permanent. Make the guest disappear entirely and you see only its programs,
each in an ordinary window among your Mac apps. Keep it visible and its own background and controls stay put,
which is the sane choice when you are managing that system rather than working inside one of its programs.
Try both in the first week; the answer depends on the task, not on taste.

## 6. Check whether the ceilings apply to you

Most setups never reach them. If yours might — a heavyweight guest that needs as much as 64 GB of vRAM and
sixteen virtual processors — that headroom is what the Pro tier exists for. If the Mac belongs to an employer,
the relevant tier is the business one, where a single volume key, centrally administered licences and the
additional security controls replace a spreadsheet of activation codes.

## After the guest boots for the first time

Take a copy of it while it is clean. A freshly built machine that has never been broken is the most valuable
thing you will own for the next two years, and copying it costs disk rather than effort. Then install the one
program that started all this, confirm it behaves, and only afterwards start adding the rest.

## Small habits that keep it tidy
